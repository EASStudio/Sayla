User: What is Newton's second law?
Assistant: Newton's second law states that force equals mass times acceleration, written as F = ma. A heavier object needs more force to accelerate at the same rate as a lighter one.

User: What is the law of conservation of energy?
Assistant: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another, like kinetic energy converting into heat through friction.

User: What is velocity?
Assistant: Velocity is the rate of change of an object's position over time, including both speed and direction, unlike speed which is direction-independent.

User: What is the difference between mass and weight?
Assistant: Mass is the amount of matter in an object and stays constant, while weight is the force of gravity acting on that mass and changes depending on gravity's strength.

User: What is kinetic energy?
Assistant: Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion, calculated as one half times mass times velocity squared.

User: What is potential energy?
Assistant: Potential energy is stored energy an object has because of its position, such as height above the ground in a gravitational field.

User: What is friction?
Assistant: Friction is a force that resists the relative motion of two surfaces in contact, converting some of that motion's energy into heat.

User: What is Newton's third law?
Assistant: Newton's third law states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction -- when one object pushes on another, the second pushes back with equal force.

User: What is momentum?
Assistant: Momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity, and in a closed system, total momentum is conserved before and after a collision.

User: What is gravity?
Assistant: Gravity is the force of attraction between any two objects with mass, and on Earth's surface it accelerates falling objects at about 9.8 meters per second squared.

User: What is an electric current?
Assistant: Electric current is the flow of electric charge, usually carried by electrons moving through a conductor, measured in amperes.

User: What is voltage?
Assistant: Voltage is the difference in electric potential energy per unit charge between two points, which drives current through a circuit.

User: What is a wave?
Assistant: A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without permanently moving matter, like sound waves moving through air.

User: What is the difference between speed and acceleration?
Assistant: Speed measures how fast something moves, while acceleration measures how quickly its speed or direction is changing.

User: What is thermal equilibrium?
Assistant: Thermal equilibrium is the state where two objects in contact reach the same temperature and heat stops flowing between them.

User: What is an electric circuit?
Assistant: An electric circuit is a closed loop that allows electric current to flow from a power source, through components, and back again.

User: What is the difference between a scalar and a vector?
Assistant: A scalar has only magnitude, like temperature, while a vector has both magnitude and direction, like velocity or force.

User: What causes tides?
Assistant: Tides are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the Moon, and to a lesser extent the Sun, pulling on Earth's oceans as the planet rotates.

User: What is pressure?
Assistant: Pressure is the amount of force applied per unit of area, often measured in pascals or pounds per square inch.

User: What is the speed of light?
Assistant: The speed of light in a vacuum is about 300,000 kilometers per second, and nothing with mass can travel that fast or faster.
